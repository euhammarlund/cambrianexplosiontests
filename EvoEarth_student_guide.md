# EvoEarth lab: student guide

Exploring the biotic and abiotic drivers of early animal evolution. Geobiology initiatives, IGN and Globe Institute, University of Copenhagen.

## What the lab is about

Animals diversified in two great pulses. The Cambrian explosion (about 539 to 510 million years ago) produced most of the animal body plans we know today. The Great Ordovician Biodiversification Event, or GOBE (about 485 to 450 million years ago), filled the seas with many more species and new ways of life.

The question for the lab is the one from Practical 3 of the lecture: what fuelled these two radiations, and were the drivers the same? Some suggested drivers are abiotic (oxygen, temperature, plate tectonics, nutrients). Others are biotic (predation, skeletons, burrowing, plankton). Many of them affect each other.

EvoEarth does not give you the answer. It helps you sort the ideas, test simple cause and effect links against data, and see how strong or weak the data are.

## What you should get out of the lab

After the lab you should be able to:

1. **Sort and connect drivers.** Sort the suggested drivers into abiotic and biotic, and explain how life and environment change each other (feedbacks).
2. **Test cause and effect in both radiations.** State a cause and effect idea, test it against data, and compare the Cambrian explosion with the GOBE.
3. **Judge the quality of the data.** Say how good the data are: the confidence grade, real data or a simplified curve, global or regional, and the time resolution.
4. **Separate correlation from causation.** Explain why two curves that move together do not prove a cause, and name other explanations, such as a common cause or better sampling.
5. **Compare hypotheses on the timeline.** Use the timeline to compare published hypotheses and say which parameters matter most and how well they are known.
6. **Argue and reflect.** Argue for a view on what drove the radiations and describe how your thinking changed.

Each goal has a matching activity, and your PDF summary shows how far you got:

| Goal | Where you practise it | What shows it in your PDF |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Sort and connect drivers | Stage 1 | Your sorting checks |
| 2 Test cause and effect | Stage 2 | Ideas tested, at least one for both radiations |
| 3 Judge data quality | Stage 2 ("How good are these data?") and Stage 3 (curves, resolution) | Data quality opened, curves or resolution changed |
| 4 Correlation and causation | Stage 2 opinions | A written reason for your opinion |
| 5 Compare hypotheses | Stage 3 (roles, both radiations) | Roles changed, both radiations visited |
| 6 Argue and reflect | Stage 4 | Your reflection |

In Stage 4 you also rate yourselves on each goal ("I can..."). Your teachers compare these ratings with what the logs show. This tells them how well the lab works. It is not a grade.

## How the lab works

You work in pairs on one laptop. EvoEarth has four stages, which you normally do in order:

| Stage | What you do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Concepts | Sort concept cards into Abiotic and Biotic | about 20 min |
| 2 Cause and effect | Suggest links between cards and check them | about 30 to 40 min |
| 3 EvoEarth explorer | Test hypotheses on the full timeline | about 45 to 60 min |
| 4 Summary | Read your summary, answer the tick questions, reflect, download the PDF and hand it in | about 10 to 15 min |

The Student guide button in the top bar opens this guide inside the app.

If you only want to use the full explorer, you can go straight to Stage 3. Press **Go straight to Stage 3: explorer** in the start box, or click Stage 3 in the top bar.

## Before the lab (about 30 minutes)

1. Look again at the lecture slides on the Cambrian explosion and the GOBE (Origins, Module 8). Note the abiotic drivers (oxygen, ozone, dispersed continents, sea-level rise, nutrients from eroded rock, the change from icehouse to hothouse, late Cambrian anoxic events) and the biotic drivers (plankton and faecal pellets, skeletons, tiering and the arms race, eyes).
2. Read Servais et al. (2009), "Understanding the Great Ordovician Biodiversification Event", GSA Today 19(4): 4 to 10. It is short and gives the main ideas for the GOBE.
3. Write down one cause you think was important for each radiation, and why. You will test it in Stage 2.
4. Check that you know these words: radiation, extinction, biodiversity, proxy, redox, anoxia, euxinia, bioturbation, tiering, suspension feeder, Lagerstätte. A short list is at the end of this guide.

## Getting started

1. Open the EvoEarth web address you got from your teachers in a normal browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari).
2. Type a group or pair name (for example "Table 4"). First names are optional. Do not type personal ID numbers.
3. Press **Start with Stage 1**, or **Go straight to Stage 3: explorer** if your teachers tell you to.
4. Keep the same browser tab open for the whole lab. Your steps are saved in a session log as you go, but only while the page is open. Do not reload or close the page before you have downloaded the PDF in Stage 4.

## Stage 1: Concepts

The 26 cards come from Christian Rasmussen's teaching on the determinants of the two radiations.

1. Hover over a card, or tap it, to read a short explanation. The explanation never tells you the camp.
2. Hover over the headings Abiotic and Biotic to read what each camp means.
3. Talk with your partner and drag the cards to the camp where you think they belong. On a touch screen, tap a card and then tap a camp. You can place a few cards or all of them.
4. You can add your own concept card. It has no answer key; discuss it with a teacher. Your own cards follow you to Stage 2, in the colour of the camp where you placed them (purple with a dashed border if you did not place them).
5. Press **Check our sorting** when you are ready. Cards in the wrong camp move to the right one, with a short reason. All checked cards are coloured: orange for abiotic, green for biotic.
6. Read the notes on cards that sit near the boundary, such as oxygen (made by photosynthesis) or burrowing (changes seawater chemistry).
7. Save at least one insight. If something does not work or is unclear, write it in the feedback box.

Questions to discuss:

- Which cards were hardest to place, and why?
- Which abiotic factors are partly made or changed by life?

## Stage 2: Cause and effect

1. Drag one or more cards to **Cause** and one or more to **Effect**. For example: Warming → Biodiversity, or Increased oxygen levels + Predation → Biodiversity. Three outcome cards are added for this stage: Biodiversity, More skeletons in the rocks, and More ways of life. Your own cards from Stage 1 are in the group "Your own cards", and you can add more here.
2. Choose whether to test the idea for the Cambrian explosion, the GOBE, or both.
3. Press **Check this idea**. The reply has four parts:
   - **Do scientists propose this?** Which of the 13 hypotheses in EvoEarth contain the link, and whether some hypotheses run the other way (a feedback). Hover over (or tap) a hypothesis name to see what it says and which authors suggested or discussed it.
   - **What do the data show?** Whether the cause and the effect moved in the same direction, and in which order. The Cambrian explosion and the GOBE are shown side by side, so you can compare them. If you test only one radiation, the other box stays empty.
   - **How good are these data?** Whether the curves are real data or simplified sketches, the confidence grade (A to D), and whether the data are global or regional.
   - **What else may be involved?** Other parameters that also move, sampling effects, and common causes.
4. The label at the top sums up the reply. Green: "Supported by hypotheses and data". Yellow: "Proposed in the literature, data mixed" (for example, the data agree for one radiation but not the other) or "Data move together, but no hypothesis proposes this". Orange: "Proposed in the literature, but data disagree", "Data do not support this idea" or "Weak or unclear". Grey: "Not testable with EvoEarth yet". The label is a starting point for discussion, not a final answer.
5. A card you made yourself has no data in EvoEarth. The reply says so; use the opinion box to argue for the link and say what data could test it.
6. Say whether you agree with the reply, and why. Then change the cards and try again. Try as many ideas as you like.
7. When an idea makes you curious, press **Explore this in Stage 3**. The explorer opens with your cause as Given and your effect as Outcome.

Tips:

- Two curves that move together do not prove that one causes the other.
- Try reversing an idea (effect as cause). Some links run both ways.
- Try combining an abiotic and a biotic cause.

## Stage 3: EvoEarth explorer

This is the full research tool. It shows the Ediacaran to Ordovician (640 to 440 million years ago) as rows of data stacked under a time scale.

What you see:

- **Rows** are parameters, ordered from the best constrained (top) to the least constrained (bottom).
- **The red bar** is the age you ask questions about. Drag it, click a plot, or type an age.
- **The letter on the right** (A to D) is the confidence grade of the data at the bar.
- **Data link** under each row opens the sources, the values and the caveats.
- **Roles** (the highlighted menu in the toolbar) decide which rows are causes (Given) and which are responses (Outcome). This is the most important control. In Guided mode you choose among presets; in Full mode you can set each row.
- **Resolution** sets the length of the time steps (bins). EvoEarth never makes a curve finer than its data. A green label means the bins rest on real data, a grey dashed label means a simplified curve, and an amber label means the curve is held at its data limit because you asked for finer steps than the data allow.
- **Curves**: some rows offer alternative curves. The diversity row has real high-resolution curves from South China and China (Fan et al. 2020; Deng et al. 2021, 2023; Zhang et al. 2021).
- **Hypotheses** on the right are ranked by how well their predictions fit the data at the bar. The heatmap shows this through time.

Suggested tasks:

1. Move the bar to 521 Ma and then to 465 Ma. Which hypotheses rank highest for each radiation? Are they the same?
2. Change the Roles preset from "Environment given" to "Biotic interactions" and to "Feedback". What changes?
3. Choose another temperature or oxygen curve. Which conclusions depend on the curve you choose?
4. In the diversity row, choose the Deng et al. 2023 curve and zoom to the Furongian. Set the resolution to 0.5 Myr. Which rows follow, and which are held at their data limit?
5. Look at the importance chart. Which parameter does most of the work, and is it well constrained?

Save insights in the notes box at the top of the explorer.

## What is recorded

EvoEarth records your group name, the first names you choose to type, the time of each step, your card moves and checks, your cause and effect ideas, the settings you change in the explorer, and your answers to the tick questions, and everything you write in the insight, opinion, reflection and feedback boxes. Nothing is sent anywhere while you work. The log exists only in the open page until you download the PDF. The PDF is not encrypted, so anyone who has the file can read it; it holds only your group name, the first names you chose to type and what you did in the lab. Your teachers read the handed-in PDFs on a teacher page that needs a login. Your teachers use the files to follow the lab and to improve EvoEarth, and the anonymised material may be used in a study of how the tool is used in teaching.

## Good habits when you work with the data

- Real data or sketch? Many curves are still simplified. The data link always tells you which.
- Global or regional? The new diversity curves come mainly from China. A pattern in one region may not be global.
- Resolution: a short event can hide between two data points that are 10 million years apart.
- Correlation is not causation: look for a common cause and for sampling effects.
- Confidence: a hypothesis that rests on grade C or D data is not well tested.

## Short word list

- **Abiotic / biotic**: not living / living, or done by living things.
- **Anoxia / euxinia**: water without oxygen / water without oxygen and with hydrogen sulfide.
- **Bioturbation**: mixing of sediment by animals.
- **Lagerstätte**: a fossil site with exceptional preservation, often of soft parts.
- **Proxy**: a measurement that stands for something we cannot measure directly, for example an isotope ratio for temperature.
- **Radiation**: a rapid increase in the number and variety of species.
- **Redox**: the oxygen and reducing state of water or sediment.
- **Tiering**: animals living at different heights above, or depths below, the sea floor.
- **Suspension feeder**: an animal that catches food particles from the water.
